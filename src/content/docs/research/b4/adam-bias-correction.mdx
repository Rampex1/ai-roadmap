Adam tracks an exponential average of the gradient and another of its elementwise square. The square average tracks magnitude, not centered variance. Both states start at zero, which initially pulls estimates toward zero. <Term id="adam-bias-correction" cap /> divides the first average by 1−β₁ᵗ and the second by 1−β₂ᵗ at step t, starting at one.

With β₁ = β₂ = 0.5 and first gradient 2, the raw states are 1 and 2. The corrected states are 2 and 4. Dividing the corrected first moment by the square root of the corrected second moment, with epsilon added after the root, gives a direction near one. Epsilon belongs outside the square root in this lesson's Adam convention.

The scalar exercise returns the raw states for the next call and the normalized direction for the present update. A learning rate is applied separately. Keeping state and direction distinct helps avoid feeding corrected estimates back into the recurrence.

## Worked example

The corrected moments recover gradient two and squared gradient four.

```python
def adam_direction(m,v,g,beta1,beta2,step,epsilon):
    from math import sqrt
    m = beta1*m+(1-beta1)*g
    v = beta2*v+(1-beta2)*g*g
    direction = (m/(1-beta1**step))/(sqrt(v/(1-beta2**step))+epsilon)
    return m,v,direction

print(adam_direction(0,0,2,0.5,0.5,1,0))
```

```text title="Output"
(1.0, 2.0, 1.0)
```

Record optimizer variant, step counter and epsilon placement when reproducing training.
