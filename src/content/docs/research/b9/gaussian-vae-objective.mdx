A variational autoencoder uses an encoder distribution q(z|x) to approximate an otherwise difficult posterior and a decoder p(x|z) to score reconstruction. The ELBO adds expected reconstruction log likelihood and subtracts a KL divergence from the encoder distribution to the prior. Minimize negative ELBO if your training loop expects a loss.

For a diagonal Gaussian posterior with mean μ and log variance ℓ and prior N(0,I), the <Term id="gaussian-vae-objective" /> is half the sum of exp(ℓ)+μ²−1−ℓ across latent coordinates. For mean zero and log variance zero, the posterior equals the prior and this term becomes zero. Parameterizing log variance keeps its exponential positive.

For reparameterization, draw ε from a standard normal independently and compute z = μ + exp(ℓ/2)ε. This moves sampling noise outside the learned arguments so gradients can flow through μ and ℓ. Keep likelihood units and reduction explicit; averaging one term by latent width while summing the other changes their relative weight.

## Worked example

Only the second coordinate has nonzero mean.

```python
def gaussian_kl(mean,log_variance):
    from math import expm1
    if len(mean)!=len(log_variance):
        raise ValueError("misaligned posterior dimensions")
    return 0.5*sum(expm1(logvar)+mu*mu-logvar for mu,logvar in zip(mean,log_variance))

print(gaussian_kl([0,2],[0,0]))
```

```text title="Output"
2.0
```
