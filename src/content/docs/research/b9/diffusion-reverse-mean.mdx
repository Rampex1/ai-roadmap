A noise-predicting DDPM learns to estimate ε from the noisy state x_t and time t. Its <Term id="diffusion-reverse-mean" /> can be written as (x_t−β_t·ε_pred/sqrt(1−ᾱ_t))/sqrt(α_t), with α_t=1−β_t. The cumulative retention controls how the noise prediction is scaled. This formula assumes a valid positive noise step and nonzero accumulated noise.

Generating samples uses a schedule in reverse, normally starting near Gaussian noise. A stochastic transition also adds a draw scaled by its chosen reverse standard deviation. The mean alone omits that step. The final transition is often sampled without extra noise in the original DDPM algorithm.

Treat the time index and variance convention as part of the implementation contract. A denoiser that predicts the clean sample or a velocity-like target requires a different conversion. Test a short deterministic trace and evaluate sample coverage and quality separately rather than relying solely on training noise MSE.

## Worked example

Even with zero predicted noise, dividing by sqrt(alpha) changes this state.

```python
def reverse_mean(state,epsilon,beta,retained):
    from math import sqrt
    if len(state)!=len(epsilon) or not 0<beta<1 or not 0<=retained<1:
        raise ValueError("invalid reverse step")
    fine = beta/sqrt(1-retained)
    return [(value-fine*guess)/sqrt(1-beta) for value,guess in zip(state,epsilon)]

print([round(x,3) for x in reverse_mean([1],[0],0.25,0.5)])
```

```text title="Output"
[1.155]
```

Fix the denoiser parameterisation and variance convention when evaluating a sampler.
